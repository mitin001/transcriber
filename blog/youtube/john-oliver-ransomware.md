# John Oliver on [ransomware](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WqD-ATqw3js)

[Transcript](https://s3.amazonaws.com/writecomments/transcripts/8f928ffac599b91b621b93c5b269dbc3.csv).

The Colonial Pipeline was breached through credential stuffing.

> The Colonial Pipeline was compromised because an employee had used the same password across multiple services, and the company did not use multifactor authentication. So, when that password was breached in an attack elsewhere, there was a direct way in.

Ransomware attacks are exceedingly common.

> Over the past few years, it's gone from a trickle to an absolute flood. The estimated total ransoms paid quadrupled to 350 million dollars last year, and that is definitely an undercount. Because companies often don't publicly disclose ransomware attacks for fear of negative press or lawsuits.

Even hospitals are not immune to ransomware attacks.

> Clinicians were forced to turn away hundreds of cancer patients who needed treatment after they lost access to medical records.

...nor thermostats.

> In 2016, hackers made the first ever ransomware for smart thermostats, cranking the temperature to 99 degrees until the owner paid up.

...nor chastity cages.

> Last year, researchers found a vulnerability in an internet-enabled chastity cage. Basically, a high-tech penis prison that could be cock-locked until the person in junk jail paid up. And interestingly, the researchers who discovered that felt compelled to go public with it because they were aware of the next product that the company was about to release.
