# [Exercises in Chaos](https://youtube.com/watch?v=1wchgrt-xts) by Rolling Stone

Lady Gaga deliberately makes wrong choices with her music just to keep it unique. The song *Disease* illustrates this idea.

> Especially with the bass on this song, I think we took a lot of kind of wrong choices in the effort of making something that felt really different from everything else that you hear. And sometimes making those wrong choices might bring an emotional life to a song that is just the best one.

Earlier in her career, she presented herself as a larger-than-life figure out of insecurity. She didn't feel like she was worthy of worldwide recognition for who she was, so she invented a person that was worthy of that.

> When I was younger I was maybe more insecure about where I was headed and that I had to kind of I had to project something larger than life because I felt so small inside and I think in a way that was like complicated but also something that really helped me connect to people all over the world.

In reality, her message was simpler. She just wasn't confident enough to state it in simple terms. She felt that without disguising it in embellishments, people wouldn't pay attention to it.

> If I'm not feeling like I can do it, maybe I can imagine a version of me that could.

Lady Gaga explains the job of an artist as being in the business of creating vibes and creating moments.

> I definitely am also in the business of making vibes, but I would say the difference between a vibe and a moment is that like a vibe can kind of happen, you know, and like you can kind of move through it, and it feels good, and you're with people, and you're like doing life, and like you're just riding the wave of the vibe.

A vibe feels good in the moment whereas a moment stays with you and changes the trajectory of your life.

> A moment is different. ... It's like a memory that you never forget. It's like when something happens that like changed you. I'm always trying to make those moments for people.

When an artist makes a seemingly wrong choice in their art, that may create a moment for someone.

> They go, like, *wow, what is that baseline?*

Concertgoers sometimes cite a moment from a Lady Gaga concert as one that changed their life.

> When I'm in front of like 80,000 people, and I'm gonna play piano, and I just kind of take a moment to like really clue in to how like two people are feeling in the front row.

Her focus on individual members of her audience allows her to create those moments.

> If I can clue into those two people I can like maybe clue into everyone. You feel the room have a moment with each other of, like, this is cool, like, I'm feeling connected to the people that I'm with. That is why I fell in love with music. Because it stopped me dead in my tracks.

She has experienced moments like that from music when she was younger, and this is what made her want to create moments like that for other people through her own music.
