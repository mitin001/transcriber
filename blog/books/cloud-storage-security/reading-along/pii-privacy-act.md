# [Personally Identifiable Information](https://www.gsa.gov/reference/gsa-privacy-program/rules-and-policies-protecting-pii-privacy-act)

Technology has advanced to the point where data that appeared to be sufficiently anonymous before can now be used to identify an individual. Anything you share on the Internet can be [linked] to everything else you've shared before. Lawmakers have caught up to this. They now recognize that anything shared on the Internet can become personally identifiable information (PII).

> Non-PII can become PII whenever additional information is made publicly available - in any medium and from any source - that, when combined with other available information, could be used to identify an individual.

For example, before facial recognition technology was sufficiently advanced, a photo of someone's face wasn't considered PII, but now there are search engines that can link a picture of anyone's face to the photos this person might've posted on social media.
