# [LexisNexis data-brokering to insurance companies](https://old.reddit.com/r/privacy/comments/1dq0446/data_broker_lexisnexis_is_selling_insurers_your/)

LexisNexis appears to have a new sales strategy: to capture the insurance industry. The service they sell to insurance companies amounts to finding excuses to raise the premiums of their customers. Because the justifications for higher premiums found by LexisNexis in the scraped private data of insurance users hold up, the insurance companies enjoy a good return on their investment in LexisNexis. Consequently, the profit margins of both the insurance and data brokering industries grow, further expanding these industries. All of this is paid for by the consumer. The hikes in insurance rates don't affect every demographic the same: it discriminates highly based on traffic congestion in their area. For example, California gets to pay an outsized share of the additional cost burden placed on the insurance industry from its capture by data brokers.

> Data broker LexisNexis is selling insurers your "real time driving data" so they can use it for lead generation to recruit desirable new clients. They sell same to insurers to set premiums. For people in CA where hard braking is unavoidable, for example, this leads to exclusion + even discrimination.

By purchasing data brokering services, what insurance companies are really buying are excuses to raise prices, engage in discrimination, etc. The question of whether it is fair to the insurance customers to impose additional costs on them based on data gathered by third-party online stalkers did not appear to have factored into the decisions the insurance industry made when partnering with the data brokering industry. Neither did the question of whether it is fair to have high-congestion areas take on more of this burden than others. All we got from insurance companies as justification for this violation of their customers' privacy was the tired old excuse surveillance states have used to strip away the rights of their subjects: it's for your own good, it's for your own safety.

> This is purely about ratcheting up prices and fucking over consumers, not safety. This is designed to milk the populace for every conceivable cent, it has nothing to do with punishing bad drivers.

When a decision is left up to an algorithm, there's no room for giving due consideration to exceptional circumstances. An [algorithm] can excuse and codify whatever sort of discrimination the algorithm designer wants.

> Your telematics unit is not a person and doesn’t have human judgement. I drive through a road that’s under construction 2-4 times a day right now. The construction zone has us driving in abnormal lanes that cause us to cross back and forth over white lines several times in different stretches. My stupid car freaks out about me not maintaining a lane. According to whatever data my car has if they’re collecting it, I’m swerving in and out of the lane willy-nilly one or two dozen times every day. I must be a terrible driver… and that’s not mentioning the time my front end telematics sensors were obscured with snow and ice and it hard braked me on a mountain highway bc it misread a falling clump of snow as a fucking car.

Corporations and governments are quick to use the excuse of safety to get you to hand over your data to them.

> There are more and more domestic violence so government should put microphones in your home if you have wife or kids, just to make sure there is no violence in your home, and while we are at it, those microphones in your home are not cheap to operate so we will sell recordings to advertisement agencies to break even.

This is a slippery slope to becoming a surveillance state.

> It’s a boiling frog situation, your privacy is slowly stripped and at first your like it is good it is for my safety, and slowly they start to track you more and more and before you realize your every move is being tracked. Some might say that China is safer place by having cameras tracking your every move everywhere because committing crime is harder.

When you live in a free country, you don't know what it feels like to live otherwise, so it's easy to [volunteer] to give up your freedom. Once given up, it's much harder to get it back. The time to fight is now, while the decision to let companies and the government spy on you is still voluntary. Opt out of it. Show them that the dissenting segment of the population is too large to ignore.
