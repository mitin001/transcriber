In her inaugural book, Fran documented cultural attitudes from the time of her youth, and many of them still persist to this day. The unyielding desire of the French to preserve their culture is one example. Turning tricks is another. We now call tricks **sugar babies**, but many of the things Fran noticed about them in the 1970s still apply.

> Tricks have feelings too, as they will be the first to tell you. If you prick them they do indeed bleed—usually your good vodka.

They like pets.

> Tricks almost always have pets. This is understandable, as everyone needs someone they can talk to on their own level.

Art is a hobby to them.

> Tricks of all regions own expensive cameras with which they take swaggeringly grainy photographs of nearby planets and sensitive young drug addicts. This is not difficult to understand, as they are relentless admirers of that which they call art and you call hobbies.

You can't make great art without it consuming your entire life for a while. Fran's life is consumed with something like art. She dedicates her life to advocating the embrace of her own undesirable traits.
