# *Social Studies*

To Fran, ideas are above all else. 

> Great people talk about ideas, average people talk about things, and small people talk about wine.

With the proliferation of [gossip] on television, in magazines, at the water cooler, and on social media, I would change the last part to: _small people talk about other people_. But let's be generous with them. Perhaps such people are simply tired of the unoriginality of ideas, so they instead choose to focus on the uniqueness of people. There is, after all, no such thing as original thought. Somebody has already thought up what you think might be an original thought. If every idea is already taken, there is no point in repeating it, and partakers in gossip have realized that before everyone else.

> Original thought is like original sin: both happened before you were born to people you could not possibly have met.

Fran chooses to be a bigger person yet. Having understood that there cannot possibly be any more original ideas, she puts novel twists on old ideas. For example, the opinion that _you look healthy_ is a bad compliment is an old idea, but Fran gives us a new reason why it might be a bad compliment.

> Telling someone he looks healthy isn’t a compliment—it’s a second opinion.

In other words, you shouldn't tell people they look healthy because it's not your place to do so if you're not a doctor. Similarly, children shouldn't be calling adults by their first names. Again, Fran explains why.

> Never allow your child to call you by your first name. He hasn’t known you long enough.

Adults shouldn't be forcing children into contractual obligations. Think back to your childhood, and imagine the problems you'd have to deal with had this happened to you.

> There isn’t an adult alive who would want to be contractually bound by a decision he came to at the age of nineteen.

Taylor Swift has a lot to say about that.
