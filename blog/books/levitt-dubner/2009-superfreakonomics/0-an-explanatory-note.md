# An Explanatory Note

A man attends a lecture by the authors of _Freakonomics_ and afterwards meets a prostitute who has the book _Freakonomics_ on her desk. He points to it and tells her that he's just met the book's authors earlier that day.

> The male instinct to impress the female is apparently strong even when the sex is already bought and paid for.

The prostitute uses this connection to write to the authors and so she makes it into their next book, _Superfreakonomics_. Networking is important, no matter your occupation. It's never about what you know but rather who you know. With the help of this prostitute among others, the freakonomists continue to tell stories to support their thesis about incentives.

> People respond to incentives, although not necessarily in ways that are predictable or manifest. Therefore, one of the most powerful laws in the universe is the law of unintended consequences.

Explore what went wrong on a number of salacious stories involving prostitutes, drug pushers, climate change doubters, and the like, and you've got yourself a book.
