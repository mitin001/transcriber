# 4. Semiconductors

An electromechanical relay closes the switch to allow the current to run through the straight wire in response to the magnetic field generated by a nearby coiled wire. When the magnetic field isn't present, the spring on the switch forces it open.

![17503918262034896283628858572446](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/618027d5-3fd5-49e6-9d42-aebb4f3a348d)

> They can only switch a limited number of times a second.

Computers built with electromechanical relays were punishingly slow.

> The Harvard Mark 1 took approximately six seconds to multiply two numbers together, so engineers started to look around for something that could switch faster.

Electromechanical relays were replaced with vacuum tubes, which were similar to lightbulbs. The genius of Edison's lightbulb was that it captured the light from a highly exothermic reaction without igniting. Edison realized that air was needed to feed the flame, so he made a lightbulb with no air inside it.

> A filament was mounted inside a glass bulb. Then all the air was sucked out, leaving a vacuum. When electricity was passed through the filament, it began to glow brightly (the vacuum stopped it from bursting into flames).

We could have gotten vacuum-tube computers 20 years earlier if only Edison had seen the potential of electricity beyond lighting up a city.

> In 1883, one of Edison’s assistants discovered that he could detect electrons flowing through the vacuum from the lighted filament to a metal plate mounted inside the bulb. Unfortunately, Edison didn’t develop this so-called Edison Effect any further. In fact, it wasn’t until 1904 that the English physicist Sir John Ambrose Fleming (1849–1945) used this phenomenon to create the first vacuum tube.

However, once a vacuum-tube computer was finally constructed by 1946 (ENIAC: Electronic Numerical Integrator and Calculator), it became quickly apparent that a more reliable and energy-efficient switching mechanism was needed. Solid-state electronics (as opposed to empy-space electronics like vacuum tubes) provided such a mechanism. Semiconductors, which are made of solids, have made computing so reliable, cheap, and fast that an information age followed their invention.

> Semiconductors can be persuaded to exhibit both conducting and insulating properties.

Silicon is the cheapest, most abundant material that can be easily modified into a semiconductor.

> Pure crystalline silicon acts as an insulator; however, scientists at Bell Laboratories in the United States found that by inserting certain impurities into the crystal lattice, they could make silicon act as a conductor.

Just as electrolytes in water make it conduct electricity, so do impurities in silicon.

> If a piece of pure silicon is surrounded by a gas containing boron or phosphorus and heated in a high-temperature oven, the boron or phosphorus atoms will permeate the crystal lattice and displace some silicon atoms without disturbing other atoms in the vicinity.

![17503918469595251561276939805570](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/d9f58c78-dfd6-4361-9cf9-021501d24474)

All data manipulations that can be done on a computer can be accomplished with a combination of many NOT and NAND gates, both of which can be readily made with MOSFETs.

> Enhancement-type devices, which are OFF unless a control signal is applied to the gate terminal to turn them ON. By comparison, depletion-type devices are ON unless a control signal is applied to turn them OFF.

Current doesn't flow through a MOSFET unless the computer applies a control signal to its gate when it needs to perform a computation involving it.

![17503918623671161572289470673526](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/d35085da-a1a1-4905-8bdd-bc9bd910fdc9)

> The drain and source form the data terminals and the gate acts as the control terminal.

In a MOSFET, a metal oxide (MO) refers to the initial material for the conductor plate of the gate for the control signal. A superconductor (S) refers to the doped silicon between the source and the drain. The field effect (FE) refers to the electromagnetic field created in the transistor (T) to allow the data current to pass through it.

> In the original devices the conducting plate was metal—hence, the term metal-oxide—but this is now something of a misnomer because modern versions tend to use a layer of polycrystalline silicon (polysilicon). When a signal is applied to the gate terminal, the plate, insulated by the oxide, creates an electromagnetic field, which turns the transistor ON or OFF —hence, the term field-effect.

Transistors like MOSFETs are made of diodes. A diode has two terminals and conducts electricity in one direction. It has long been known that some diodes emit light when a voltage is applied across them. Now that materials to make light-emitting diodes (LEDs) for each RGB color have been discovered (red aluminum gallium arsenide, green gallium phosphide, and blue gallium nitride), they can fully replace incandescent light bulbs.

> They have a very long life (typically 100,000 hours as compared to 1000 hours for an incandescent light bulb), they generate very pure, saturated colors, and they are extremely energy efficient (LEDs use up to 90% less energy than an equivalent incandescent bulb).

The display technology is constantly evolving in terms of energy efficiency, to the point where displays use organic compounds and no longer even require [backlight].

> When current flows through the LED, positive and negative charges combine and light is emitted. An Organic Light-Emitting Diode (OLED) performs the same trick, but it is based on thin layers of organic molecules.

Passive devices (like resistors, capacitors, inductors, or diodes) merely respond to electrical signals. Passively. On the other hand, a transistor is an active device.

> A component that can use an electrical signal to control the current passing through it.

Once we introduce active components into a circuit, it is no longer simply an electric circuit, but rather an electronic one.

> In order for a circuit to be properly called “electronic, ” it must contain one or more active devices. On this basis, a circuit comprising only resistors, capacitors, and inductors would be considered to be an “electric circuit ” rather than an “electronic circuit.”
