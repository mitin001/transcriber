#  9. How It Feels to Live Free of Roles and Fantasies

Religion often demands purity of thought. This is how religion exercises emotional blackmail over its subjects. When anyone tells you "don't even think that," that's what it is.

> Having a thought or feeling isn’t initially under your control. You don’t plan to think or feel things; you just do. Think of it this way: Your thoughts and feelings are an organic part of nature expressing itself through you. Nature isn’t going to be dishonest about how you feel, and you don’t have a choice about what thoughts nature brings up in you. Accepting the truth of your feelings and thoughts doesn’t make you a bad person. It makes you a whole person, and mature enough to know your own mind.

When a thought occurs to you, you decide what to do with it. Until it occurs, you have no control over it and could not have possibly prevented it from occurring since you had no way of knowing it was coming. A random thought is a reflection of your [inner self]. The only way you can influence which thoughts occur to you is through building your inner world, something control freaks like religion and overbearing parents do not want you to do. They only want you to feel guilty for thinking the thoughts they wouldn't approve of, and guilt is your tether to them. Leaving a religion or breaking off contact with your parent may be a taboo thought, but, like any thought, it is permissible, and you are free to debate this question.

> Your first job is your own self-care, including setting limits on how much you give, even to the point of suspending contact with your parents if necessary.

Once you build up your [inner world], you may not even need to avoid your overbearing parents or religious types or anyone. A person in touch with their inner self isn't bothered by their presence. They do not feel guilty for where their priorities lie.

> By giving up your healing fantasy about changing your parents, you let them be who they are. When they’re no longer under pressure to change, they may be able to treat you differently—or not. Your job is to be okay either way.

Going into a difficult situation, remember that it is up to you to emerge from it psychologically unscathed. Always have a backup plan in a [negotiation]. Never let your emotions wound you.
