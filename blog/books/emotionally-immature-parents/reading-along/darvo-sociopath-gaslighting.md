# [A Defense Against Gaslighting Sociopaths](https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2025/04/darvo-sociopath-gaslighting/682372/)

The author explains the DARVO technique: deny, accuse, reverse victim offender.

> When confronted with evidence of their misconduct, abusers typically begin by denying or minimizing culpability (“You’re making a big deal out of nothing”), before accusing the victim of creating the problem (“You regret what happened, and now you’re blaming me”) and then reversing the roles of victim and offender (“You should really be the one apologizing”).

Sociopaths use this technique to gain power over their victims. It starts with misbehavior and escalates into gaslighting.

> This type of person-to-person psychological warfare is designed to deflect any penalty for misbehavior, and turn it instead into an opportunity to gain power over you.

When confronted with a misbehaving adult, call him out on it. Watch his reaction. If he does not own his actions, you may be dealing with a gaslighting sociopath.

> A perpetrator of harm, when called out for it, plays the victim by claiming that their action has been maliciously misrepresented.

We have created an environment in which sociopaths thrive. Nowadays, people walk around in a constant state of being offended.

> We now inhabit a “culture of offense,” a way of turning a claim that some behavior or statement is offensive into, in effect, a right to be offended, which creates a further claim of victimhood.

This is bad for the culture.

> This culture can be used as a cudgel to disingenuously keep disfavored views and voices out of the public realm.

For example, a loud, obnoxious Karen with COVID will refuse to wear a mask or even cover her cough and thus spread her illness onto the people who didn't get vaccinated because people like her have also made illness prevention on the whole a disfavored practice. Karens are a loud minority. 7% of the population exhibit the Dark Triad personality traits.

> Three negative personality traits: narcissism (it’s all about me), Machiavellianism (I’m willing to hurt you to get what I want), and psychopathy (I feel no empathy for you and no remorse when I victimize you).

Some researchers add a fourth trait to the Dark Triad: sadism.

> Gaslighting is also strongly tied to sadism; in other words, when an abuser’s duplicitousness causes you confusion and misery, they get a kick out of that. Sadism is such a persistent feature of this personality type, in fact, that some scholars regard it as the fourth trait of a Dark Tetrad character.

People exhibiting these traits cannot be cured. Psychopathy is incurable.

> Dark Triad traits—especially when full-blown psychopathy is involved—are almost impossible to remedy.

Their brains are different. They're missing the areas we rely on for factoring empathy into our decision making.

> These people have lower brain volume than the general population in areas related to emotional regulation (the dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, the cingulate cortex) and reward (the ventral striatum, the orbitofrontal cortex).

For normal people, if we do something that hurts another person but benefits us, we tend to not repeat this behavior. The remorse from the hurt we caused others cancels out the positive reinforcement from the benefit to us. The people incapable of feeling remorse only get the positive reinforcement from such an interaction, and they're likely to repeat it.

> Their psychopathic tendencies make them incapable of remorse: If you’re not sorry for doing something, and it yielded the results you sought, you will go on doing it.

Sociopaths do not prey on other sociopaths. They seek out the most empathetic among us because those are the people most susceptible to fall for their tricks.

> For someone subjected to the technique, questioning whether the misbehavior really happened, or whether they somehow misjudged the situation, is actually an indicator of their emotional health. The very thing that makes you a decent human, in other words, is what the perpetrator seizes upon to induce doubt and self-blame. Susceptibility to this moral self-questioning is what DARVO aims to exploit.

The Internet provides an excellent opportunity to diagnose a sociopath. Watch their social media interactions. If they troll people, stay away.

> Identify a DARVO user before establishing a romance, friendship, or work partnership. One way to do this is by observing a person’s internet activity, if you can. People with Dark Triad characteristics have been found to be especially drawn to what clinicians euphemistically call “problematic social-media use.” Someone who’s always getting into online fights, let alone trolling or doxxing, may be inclined toward the DARVO jiu-jitsu. And if you discover that they are gaslighting people online, you can bet they will gaslight you in real life. Beware and cut ties if you can.

For example, if there's anything unkind on a dating profile, stay away. This is this person's best effort to come across as normal. It's only downhill from there. Also beware the sociopaths that try to hide their psychopathy.

> Dark Triads can present as charming and captivating, because they tend to be more invested in getting you to love them first than they are in establishing the basis of a mutually loving partnership.

They won't do a good-enough job, and you will see right through them — unless you're lying to yourself and seeing the world through rose-colored glasses. Falling in love or desperation can do that to you.
