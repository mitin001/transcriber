# 7: Nurturing Your Relationship with Yourself

People may think it's charming to put yourself down. Self-deprecating comedians like Joan Rivers make it look like a sure way to get the audience on their side. Remember, real life isn't Hollywood.

> Refuse to judge and criticize yourself.

Nothing's to be gained from empty self-criticism.

> Criticizing yourself won’t improve you any more than attacking a child’s self-esteem makes them more confident. Self-criticism is no way to have a relationship with yourself. It sentences you to a life of anxious dependency in which no power is greater than someone else’s opinion of you. Instead of judging yourself, why not think about what you’d like to change, figure out the steps to get there, and seek support?

When you put yourself down, you repeat the things emotionally immature people (EIPs) in your life have said to you to try to manipulate you into [enmeshing] with them. They did so to try to disconnect you from your [inner self].

> EIPs dismiss your inner world as if it were unnecessary and irrelevant. If you believe these dismissals, you will miss the wisdom your inner self offers you in the form of feelings, intuitions, and insights.

Without a strong connection to your inner self, you will feel lost. [Psychiatric symptoms] will creep up on you, and you won't know why. You won't know how to fix it. Your inner self is the guiding light through life's challenges.

> Establish a more trusting, respectful relationship with your inner self and its guidance.

If you abandon it for too long, your inner self will eventually resort to causing you mental distress to try to get your attention.

> Many people don’t feel entitled to self-protection and instead smolder in resentment. Unfortunately, resentment is a passive reaction and doesn’t help you protect or care for yourself. Self-protective instincts can be scary at first because they come in the form of such strong feelings: indignation, outrage, or even hatred. But these are just emotional signals that someone has tried to control or dominate you. These feelings are telling you your inner experiences matter and must be protected.

When someone tries to wrestle your freedom and independence away from you, they often do it covertly so as not to attract attention to what they're actually doing. Many people indeed don't notice that they're being manipulated into submission until it's too late. However, even if they miss the red flags, they still feel _off_, perhaps strongly so. This is their inner selves warning them to walk away. The more in touch you are with your inner self, the sooner you get yourself out of a bad situation, the better equipped you are to move on from it. This is why strengthening a connection to your inner self is important. One way to do so is by broadening your inner world through learning and imagination. Another is through meditation. When the outer world gets overwhelming, meditation is an escape into your inner world. The more you visit your inner world, the more in touch you are with your inner self.

> Meditation reveals that once you detach from the outer world, there is a spacious inner realm inside you that is uniquely alive and replenishing to experience. Meditation gives you direct confirmation of the inner world’s reality.
