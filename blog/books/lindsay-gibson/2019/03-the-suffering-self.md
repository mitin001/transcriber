# 3: Longing for a Relationship with Your EI Parent

Some think that people are generally good. They only see good in everyone, which is a sort of perspective that could put them in great danger. What these people are doing is projecting their goodness onto others. Their parents may have been emotionally immature (EI), and so they needed to see good in them to get through their difficult childhood.

> Adult children of EI parents are especially likely to assume others are psychologically similar, seeing too much maturity or potential in people. It’s okay to give someone the benefit of the doubt, but not to the point where you expect them to show behaviors they are incapable of. This overoptimism is a habit formed early in life when a child needed to believe in a parent’s goodness.

People who see good in everyone often grow up to be passive and powerless. They only know how to please others and suffer in silence.

> As a child with EI parents, some part of yourself may have figured out that the best way to get along was to suffer in silence and not rock the boat.

As it is with learning to project own goodness onto others, the suffering self also formed in childhood.

> Rather than feeling anger or knowing what it wants, the suffering self stays stuck in chronic unhappiness and feelings of helplessness. It had to relinquish assertiveness as it tried to adjust to a difficult childhood situation.

It is time to transform your suffering self into your true self by abandoning the role of a martyr for others' causes. [Do not play roles] for the sake of others. Live authentically.

> The suffering self convinces you that self-sacrifice makes you a good person or at least more likely to be loved by others. But now this suffering self should be retired as the model for your relationships. Being active on your own behalf is much better than passivity and helplessness.
