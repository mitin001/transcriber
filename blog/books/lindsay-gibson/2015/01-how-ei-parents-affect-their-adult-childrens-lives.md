# 1. How Emotionally Immature Parents Affect Their Adult Children’s Lives

Lindsay C. Gibson tells the story of Meaghan, who was pushed into a loveless marriage by the emotionally immature people surrounding her. Now she was stuck and unhappy, with no one around her willing to let her develop her inner world, much less understand it.

> Her family didn’t want to hear her. Her husband and parents weren’t trying to understand; they were focused on trying to convince her she was mistaken.

When arguing with someone, your objective shouldn't be to win the argument at all costs. Try to understand the other party and find common ground. Conversations are not necessarily exchanges of information. They are primarily [exchanges of attention]. You don't win on an interpersonal level when you win an argument. Emotionally immature people will want to win an argument at all costs, even when they're wrong. Emotionally immature parents will keep shutting down their children's interests and instead put forth rules and expect them to live their lives by those rules. Children with such emotionally stunted childhoods focus on appeasing their parents rather than developing their inner selves. They grow up to be people pleasers.

> The most primitive parts of our brain tell us that safety lies in familiarity.

They derive a feeling of safety from serving others, and there will be many people trying to take advantage of them. They will choose abusive partners rather than those with whom they could find true intimacy.

> We gravitate to situations we have had experience with because we know how to deal with them.

Links between the inner worlds of two people are what make up [true intimacy]. Not only will an emotionally neglected child gravitate toward manipulative people in adulthood, but they will also have an insufficiently developed inner worlds, limiting their chances for linking with the inner worlds of others and finding true intimacy.
