# 9: Updating Your Self-Concept

Learning a technical skill is one way to show ourselves that we are important, even if our parents failed to do so on account of their emotional immaturity. When you're the only person in the room that can solve an important problem for your business or your patient, that certainly convinces you on your importance. So, not only does learning new skills broaden your [inner world], it also heals your emotional trauma. Another helpful thing to repair your self-concept is to understand and disregard feelings of shame.

> See shame as a helpful warning that someone is trying to make you feel bad so they can feel better about themselves. Seeing shame for what it is not only helps free you from others’ emotional coercions but repairs your self-concept as well.

[Guilt and shame] are temporary signals to correct your behavior or examine. Both should be fleeting, not lasting emotions. Let go of guilt and shame by exploring what caused them and rethinking your relationships with the people who hoisted those unpleasant emotions onto you.

> Repeatedly exploring childhood shame feelings and then relabeling them correctly—as highly unpleasant emotions, not the truth of you—shrinks shame back into a manageable emotion, not a declaration of your worth as a human being. By questioning shame, you can unmask it as just an emotion foisted on you by others, instead of being your core identity. Instead of absorbing shame into your self-concept, you could treat it as just another emotion.

Shame is a tool of an emotionally immature person (EIP) used against psychologically defenseless people. Understand this so you can feel empathy for yourself rather than perpetuate shameful feelings. "I'm so ashamed" should become "they had no right to shame me but did it anyway, so I need to reduce and [carefully manage] interactions with them."

> The only truth about shame is that an EIP probably made you feel awful about yourself at an age when you were psychologically defenseless. As an adult, you can defuse shame feelings by exposing the erroneous self-belief that underlies it, looking at it without running away, and then questioning it.

Emotionally immature people use dirty tricks like shame in their emotional coercions because they lack the capacity to achieve a true emotional understanding of themselves or others. These are the same people that regard [empty, vapid pronouncements] as wisdom and reduce human beings to [props] on their stage. They can't grasp individual differences and the rich complexity of each person's inner world, so they reduce people to types. They assign a person a personality type (e.g., INFP) or a zodiac sign (e.g., Scorpio) and rely on it to justify it to others that they know the person better than they know themselves.

> They lump people together, seeing them as much more similar than they really are. Individual complexities are ignored as they say things like, “You’re just like your father!” or “You’re like my side of the family.” They think they know you because you remind them of someone. As a result, they give you a typecast self-concept that doesn’t really fit you. With EI parents, you were told what to be, not helped to discover who you are. But now as an adult, you can expand your self-concept to include all your potential and complexity, even if EIPs still pigeonhole you in an oversimplified or childlike identity. You don’t have to go through life feeling like less than you are. Thankfully, your self-concept no longer has to be held hostage by your parents’ opinions. You are now free to discover who you are and what you want to become. You can update your self-concept to fit who you really are.
