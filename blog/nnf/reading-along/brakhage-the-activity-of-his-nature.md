# Stan Brakhage. [The Activity of His Nature](https://monoskop.org/images/f/fd/James_David_E_ed_Stan_Brakhage_Filmmaker.pdf)

In this essay, David James argues that Stan Brakhage made the kind of visionary film he made because that was his nature.

> Milton produced *Paradise Lost* for the same reason that a silk worm produces silk. It was an activity of his nature.

Brakhage proposed that there do not need to be any movies beyond our home movies.

> ...proposed the sufficiency of a fundamentally domestic cinema, an art version of home movies in which family life and the filmmaking it engendered were lived as an aesthetic process.

We do not need any more from film than to have it communicate to others our domestic life, Brakhage seemed to believe. This is understandable from his point of view because he has transformed the medium of filmmaking itself into an art form, no matter what the film has captured.

> He has given "to cinema an intelligence and subtlety that is usually the province of the older arts."
