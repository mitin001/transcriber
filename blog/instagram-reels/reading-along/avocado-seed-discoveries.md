# [Avocado seed discoveries](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9789361/)

By discarding avocado seeds, we pollute the environment and mindlessly deny ourselves their nutritional benefits.

> The processing industry discards avocado seeds, which increases production and ultimately pollutes the environment. It would be advantageous to handle these waste by-products both economically and environmentally.

By discarding the avocado seed, we throw away 15% of this expensive and valuable fruit. 100 g of avocado seeds contain 10 mg of vitamin A and almost 100 mg of ascorbic acid (an antioxidant but not the same thing as vitamin C, of which there is very little in an avocado seed).

> Seeds of avocados represent a substantial percentage (13 %–17 %) of the avocado fruit and are rich in various functional and bioactive components, namely polysaccharides, proteins, lipids, minerals, and vitamins.

Because of the richness and diversity of these compounds, avocado seed flour is highly absorbent and soluble.

> The flour has good water and oil absorption capacity and solubility (2.4 %, 2.16 %, 11.2 %, respectively) related to different macro and micronutrients present in the seeds and their strong intermolecular interactions.

Avocado seeds are non-toxic.

> They are a good natural source of biologically active ingredients for the food, pharmaceutical, and cosmetic sectors because they contain no harmful or dangerous compounds.

Avocado seeds are mostly made of starch. 

> Among all the macromolecules found in avocado seeds, carbohydrates are said to make up a significant portion (64.9 %). Starch makes up 91.2 % of the total carbohydrates in avocado seeds.

The wellness industry will tout phenolics to sell you blueberries. Avocado seeds are a better source of phenolics.

> Avocado seeds contain severalfold phenolics compared to popular antioxidant sources such as raw blueberry.

Chemotherapy can be an effective way to treat cancer, but it is toxic to all cells. Plant-based anti-cancer compounds may be less potent, but they do not harm other cells. People eating plant-based diets are more resilient to cancer.

> Synthetic anti-tumor medications have been found in clinical research to have possible therapeutic results but substantial toxicity to normal cells, posing a threat to human health. Due to its safety and immune-enhancing effect in humans, plant sources are gaining interest as anti-tumor medicines with lower toxicity.

Research into anti-cancer compounds from avocado seeds shows promise for developing safer anti-cancer therapeutics from this otherwise discarded plant material.

> Polyphenols from avocado seeds can inhibit human prostate cancer cells (LNCaP), breast cancer cells (MCF7), lung cancer cells (H1299), and colon cancer cells (HT29) with inhibition rates of 19, 19.1, 67.6, and 132.2 μg/mL in a dose-dependent manner.

Polyphenols can arrest the cell cycle of cancer cells so their division is no longer uncontrolled.

> Avocado seed extracts induced G0/G1 cell cycle arrest via downregulating cyclin D1 and E2 expression in prostate cancer cells.

Cancer cells lose their function but instead of going into apoptosis, they keep dividing, amplifying the diseased mutations. Compounds in avocado seeds can help such cells find their way to apoptosis.

> Seed extracts (0.1 mg/mL) increased activation of caspase-3 and caspase-3 target protein, poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP), resulting in apoptosis. Ethanolic extracts of avocado seeds induced apoptosis in Jurkat lymphoblastic leukemia cells in an oxidative stress-dependent manner through depolarization of the mitochondrial membrane, activating protease caspase-3, and transcription factor p53, and predominancy of apoptosis-inducing factor.

Even metabolism byproducts of the compounds in avocado seeds (e.g., triterpenoid) retain their anti-cancer properties.

> These secondary metabolites disrupt the membrane permeability of the mitochondrial cell wall, resulting in cell necrosis.

Lipids found in avocado seeds can simulate cytokine release.

> Lipidic extracts encouraged the release of cytokines IL-6, IL-8, and IL-10; but inhibited IL-1β secretion.

Some compounds in avocado seeds are anti-diabetic, i.e., they lower the blood glucose probably by helping to convert glucose into glycogen for storage in the liver.

> The blood-glucose-lowering effect was attributed to bioactive compounds that assist in depositing glucose into the glycogen in the liver cells.

The cholinergic hypothesis states that Alzheimer's disease (AD) results from the breakdown of acetylcholine in the brain. Compounds in avocado seeds can inhibit the action of the enzymes catalyzing this breakdown: acetylcholinesterase and butyrylcholinesterase.

> Reducing acetylcholine levels may inhibit brain transmission in Alzheimer's patients.

Even if the cholinergic hypothesis is wrong, increasing the levels of acetylcholine in the brain can help manage the disease.

> This ultimately improves the communication between the nerve cells and helps to manage neurodegenerative ailments such as AD.

Fatty acids can be antimicrobial because they disintegrate cell membranes (which are also made of fatty acids).

> The fatty acids of avocado seeds display antimicrobial activities. The fatty acid has double bonding in the cis-configuration. They alter its functionality by disordering the phospholipid chain, resulting in fluidity, disorganization, and disintegration of the cell membrane resulting in leakage of intracellular content and cell death.

This is akin to prions misfolding the proteins with which they come in contact.
