# [Anecdotes and Data](https://marketoonist.com/2024/06/anecdotes-and-data.html)

When we look at many anecdotes, generalizable patterns emerge.

> In the late 60s, a Cal Professor and political scientist named Ray Wolfinger heard a student dismiss a statement as “just an anecdote” and responded that “the plural of anecdote is data.”

However, anecdotes are not necessarily just unstructured data. They can also just be opinions. Those anecdotes should be discarded before they add unnecessary noise into the data.

> Anecdotes can be valuable shorthand, but they can also be limiting, biased, or totally wrong.  Anecdotes are sometimes thinly veiled opinions. At the other end of the spectrum, anecdotes can be a form of unstructured data that can lead to interesting insights.  

The founding CEO of Netscape also has an opinion on data vs. opinions.

> If we have data, let’s look at data. If all we have are opinions, let’s go with mine.
