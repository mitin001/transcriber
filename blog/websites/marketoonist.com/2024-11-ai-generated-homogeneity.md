# [AI-Generated Homogeneity](https://marketoonist.com/2024/11/ai-generated-homogeneity.html)

![17515133542835432806411647108637](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/7ebdee1d-1da5-4a28-8bc4-f97a7d6541f7)

AI has a bias toward homogeneity.

> The tendency for AI results to look alike. As AI predicts what to generate, the path of least resistance is an averaging of the content in its source material.

AI output all looks the same. There's a great samening across AI users.

> The researchers studied an AI model trained on images of different breeds of dogs. The source material included a naturally wide variety of dogs (French Bulldogs, Dalmatians, Corgis, Golden Retrievers, etc.). But when asked to generate an image of a dog, the AI model typically returned the more common dog breeds (Golden Retrievers) and less frequently the rarer breeds (French Bulldogs). Over time, the cycle reinforces and compounds when future generations of AI models are trained on these outputs. It starts to forget the more obscure dog breeds entirely. Soon it only creates images of Golden Retrievers.

As AI outputs flood the Internet, AI will crawl more and more of its own outputs, leading to model collapse.

> The LLM is trained so much on AI-generated Golden Retriever images that the results turn nonsensical and stop looking like dogs at all.

Use it to your advantage. While everyone is turning out AI-generated content, all of which looks the same, create unique non-AI content to differentiate yourself in the marketplace.

> This baseline situation creates opportunities for those who can push against this status quo. Homogeneity is ultimately at odds with distinctiveness.

Those who want to solve the AI problem with more AI are missing the point.

> You can’t break through the clutter by adding to it.
