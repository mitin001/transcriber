# [Colleges seek to ‘ChatGPT-proof’ assignments](https://apnews.com/article/chatgpt-cheating-ai-college-1b654b44de2d0dfa4e50bf0186137fc1)

AI makes people lazy. Why learn anything when AI can think for you? 

> The use of research tools like library databases declined notably following the emergence of chatbots.

Looking for an answer to your question in a library is different from asking a chatbot for the answer. Using a library immerses you into the field and provides the context for your question whereas a chatbot may give you a satisfactory answer with errors you wouldn't know or bother to notice. To see just how lazy an AI user is, look at the students that leave blatant AI artifacts in the work they don't think twice about turning in to their teachers.

> I had answers come in that said, ‘I am just an AI language model, I don’t have an opinion on that.’

And if teachers outsource grading to AI, which can't catch AI cheaters, they will create a situation where the laziest among their students will pass the class and be unleashed into the world where a decline in information quality means a diminishment of the sole advantage humans have over other species.

> Educators also worry about what students lose by skipping steps.

Children raised by AI will not have the skills they would need to process information as adults. They will not be able to think for themselves.

> Intro-level students taking AI shortcuts are cheating themselves out of skills.

Expect teachers rattled by this realization to overcorrect on AI-proofing their classes. To prove you did not cheat on your assignments, retain your drafts. Version your work. Demonstrate that the polished deliverable you turned in has evolved significantly over time and wasn't just handed to you by a chatbot.

> Some professors will be requiring students to show editing history and drafts to prove their thought process.
