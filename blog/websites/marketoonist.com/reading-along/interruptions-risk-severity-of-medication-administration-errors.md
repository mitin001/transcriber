# [Association of interruptions with an increased risk and severity of medication administration errors](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20421552/)

By interrupting a nurse performing a procedure, you're increasing the risk of failure of such a procedure. For example, if a nurse sterilizing medical equipment gets interrupted, there's an increased risk that the equipment is still contaminated, which could cause an infection in the next patient.

> Each interruption was associated with a 12.1% increase in procedural failures and a 12.7% increase in clinical errors.

Clinical errors include administering an incorrect dose of a drug. Overdoses can lead to poisoning, which can be life-threatening.

> Error severity increased with interruption frequency. Without interruption, the estimated risk of a major error was 2.3%; with 4 interruptions this risk doubled to 4.7%.

The more interruptions there are in a healthcare setting, the higher the chance of a catastrophic failure. Hiring more experienced staff does not help. Only reducing interruptions can help.

> Nurse experience provided no protection against making a clinical error.

Interruptions affect more experienced nurses just as much as they do the less experienced ones.
