# [Signs of AI writing](https://en.m.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)

AI-generated text is a hodgepodge of tired old tricks inexperienced writers (e.g., school children) use to appear like they've understood the assignment. They will reuse words from the assignment to make it appear like they're answering the question and keep mentioning the assigned topic in a vacuous way, adding nothing with each mention.

> AI writing often puffs up the importance of the subject matter with reminders that it represents or contributes to a broader topic. There seems to be only a small repertoire of ways that it writes these reminders.

AI writing is vulnerable to exposure because it embeds telltale signs of its use into its output. Watch for these phrases.

> is/stands as/serves as a testament, plays a vital/significant role, underscores its importance, continues to captivate, leaves a lasting impact, watershed moment, key turning point, deeply rooted, profound heritage, steadfast dedication, stands as a, solidifies ...

For example, ask AI to write an encyclopedia article, and it will write a sales pitch, trying to sell you on why the object of the article is noteworthy.

> AI has serious problems keeping a neutral tone, especially when writing about something that could be considered "cultural heritage" – in which case it will constantly remind the reader that it is cultural heritage.

Encyclopedia readers already know that the object of the article is noteworthy. Otherwise, there wouldn't be an article written about it. They came for the facts, not to be annoyed with an amateur's effort at writing marketing materials for it.

> Buzzwords and general vapidness.

The tells of an AI response to a prompt asking it to write an encyclopedia article are words that inappropriately oversell the significance of the object of the article.

> Words to watch: rich cultural heritage, rich history, breathtaking, must-visit, must-see, stunning natural beauty, enduring/lasting legacy, rich cultural tapestry ...

Watch for sentences with unnecessary phrases that begin with the -ing word (e.g., improving, highlighting, emphasizing).

> AI chatbots tend to superficially comment on or analyze information, often in relation to its significance, recognition, or impact. This is often done by attaching a present participle ("-ing") phrase at the end of sentences, sometimes with vague attributions to third parties.

Present participle phrases betray a hacky writer, aching to be done with the assignment. Or, they are yet another tell of AI-generated text.

> Consumers benefit from the flexibility to use their preferred mobile wallet at participating merchants, improving convenience.

Do mobile wallets really improve convenience? Whose convenience do they improve? Offer the evidence supporting this claim and clarify it, or omit the vague present participle phrase.

> These comments are generally unhelpful and introduce unnecessary or fictional opinions.

An opinion without evidence is empty prose taking up space on the page and wasting its reader's time.

> The Federation was internationally recognized and invited to participate in the Asia Pickleball Summit, highlighting Pakistan’s entry into the global pickleball community.

An invitation to participate in a summit is hardly the milestone the mindless chatbot is hyping it up to be. Pakistan was already part of the pickleball community, otherwise it wouldn't have been invited to its summit.
