# [Mehdi Ordikhani-Seyedlar: What happens in your brain when you pay attention](https://www.ted.com/talks/mehdi_ordikhani_seyedlar_what_happens_in_your_brain_when_you_pay_attention/transcript)

To find out what someone is looking at or paying attention to, have the different images flicker at different rates. The brain will resonate with the flickering rate of the image it is paying attention to.

> Depending on which of these flickers you are paying attention to, certain parts of your brain will start resonating in the same rate as that flickering rate. So by analyzing your brain signals, we can track where exactly you are watching or you are paying attention to.

Someone with ADHD can't be tested this way because they would be too distracted by the flickering of the surrounding images and constantly switch their attention between them. Their brains can't focus on something when something else is trying to get their attention. They can't filter out the competing signal.

> The filtering ability of the brain is indeed a key for attention, which is missing in some people, for example in people with ADHD. So a person with ADHD cannot inhibit these distractors, and that's why they can't focus for a long time on a single task. But what if this person could play a specific computer game with his brain connected to the computer, and then train his own brain to inhibit these distractors?

If the brain's filtering ability is weak in someone with ADHD, then it can be strengthened like a muscle, with persistent exercise and practice. A video game that teaches one to resist distractions could be one way to help people exercise their way out of having ADHD.
