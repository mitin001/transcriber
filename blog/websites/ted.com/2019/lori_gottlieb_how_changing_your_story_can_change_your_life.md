# [Lori Gottlieb: How changing your story can change your life](https://www.ted.com/talks/lori_gottlieb_how_changing_your_story_can_change_your_life/transcript)

When a friend comes to you with a problem, [don't try to solve it] for them. Listen attentively, ask questions, and offer compassion. Try to avoid restoring to **idiot compassion**, though.

> In idiot compassion, we go along with the story, we say, "You're right, that's so unfair," when a friend tells us that he didn't get the promotion he wanted, even though we know this has happened several times before because he doesn't really put in the effort, and he probably also steals office supplies.

Even more importantly, avoid engaging in **ultracrepidarianism**.

> The habit of giving advice or opinions outside of one's knowledge or competence.

Lori Gottlieb has the word _ultracrepidarianism_ on a sticky note on her desk to remind her that she must resist the temptation to try to solve people's problems but instead give them the tools they need to solve them themselves.

> As a therapist, I can help people to sort out what they want to do, but can't make their life choices for them. Only you can write your story, and all you need are some tools.

Therapists like Gottlieb are trained in empowering people, but you don't have to be a trained therapist to make a difference in their situation. Asking questions helps the person think through the problem, and talking through it helps soften the blow of their situation on them. A grief shared is a grief halved.
