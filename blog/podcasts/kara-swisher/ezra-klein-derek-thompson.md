# [Ezra Klein and Derek Thompson](https://s3.amazonaws.com/writecomments.com/transcripts/746ea55bfcede9c50bec786f3bc1a266.csv)

Ezra Klein and Derek Thompson begin their book _Abundance_ with a bold exercise in blame throwing.

> The future could be ours if only liberals would get out of the way.

Kara interprets this as a call to shame liberals by liberals.

> Shame on us, in other words.

Kara distills the point of the book to trying to get liberals to be more tolerant of laxer regulation for a good cause: to get things built.

> You blame the left in a lot of this book for making it too hard to build the things we need.

When the left is unwilling to do this, the right gets elected.

> Of the $42 billion that we allocated or authorized for broadband construction to help rural and low-income Americans, practically none of it was built. And I was talking to this guy who was very deeply involved in broadband construction in the South, and he said for months, for years, it was taking so much time to fill out the paperwork and send it to Commerce and get replies from Commerce and then fix the replies from Commerce and then send back the paperwork until suddenly the calendar flips from 2021 to 2024. Practically nothing is built. And this bill that was supposed to be an advertisement for the Biden administration instead becomes a cudgel that the Trump administration or Trump campaign uses to beat them over the head with.

The right has become a dangerous movement, and letting them beat the left will make it complicit in the damage that follows.

> One way that you sideline more dangerous political movements is you prove out the success of your own. That you can build things.

The left needs to understand that they are fighting not just for the everyday concerns of their constituents but also for immigration and transgender rights. If these groups of people to be included in the American experiment, it is important for the left to win against the right's politics of cruelty.

> The cruelties Trump followers submit to in order to prove their loyalty.

The authors call out the right's corruption when it comes to the protection of the environment. For example, the Clean Air Act and Clean Water Act as advocating for the opposite of what their names would lead you to believe them to be.

> It was about making it easier to dump pollution in the air and the water. There is an Orwellianness to this.

The authors call out the right's corruption when it comes to efficiency.

> Efficiency needs a goal.

Neither Democrats no Republicans seem to understand what efficiency means. Democrats think it's bureaucracy while Republicans think it's Elon Musk's business.

> If you want a government that can be more efficiently taken over by Elon Musk's companies, then yeah, DOGE is doing great.

The Republicans have given Elon Musk free reign when it comes to dismantling government, and so he started reshaping it in the way that only serves him.
