# [30](https://s3.amazonaws.com/writecomments.com/transcripts/16b07f7810caacdf2a0d16007ae44c04.csv). Synthetic Fraud

Identity theft has become harder for criminals to pull off. Valid social security numbers are no longer easy for them to guess.

> If you're issued a social security number prior to 2011, if I have the last four of your social security number and I know the state you're born in and the year that you're born, it's very easy for me to get the first five. So the Social Security Administration, they randomized the numbers so that that type of identity theft could no longer work.

Because identity theft has become harder, criminals have had to adapt. Now they create synthetic identities and commit crimes on behalf of the people they've made up. Synthetic fraud thrives in our highly vulnerable financial markets. One of the vulnerabilities enabling this sort of crime is systemic racism.

> Credit reports don't recognize rental payments. They don't recognize utility payments for the most part. They don't recognize cell phone payments. These are things that would help low-income neighborhoods, black neighborhoods, Hispanic neighborhoods. That's not reported on the FICO score. Rent payments aren't reported. Meanwhile, mortgage payments are reported. So that continues to drive down the ability, the creditability of people in low-income neighborhoods, okay, of people of color.

Criminals create synthetic identities and then use them to buy basic necessities (cars, furniture, utilities, etc) and sell it to low-income individuals with low credit scores under predatory terms (CPNs).
