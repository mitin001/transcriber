# [29](https://s3.amazonaws.com/writecomments.com/transcripts/a0ffc444acadcdae4cf00cffd209e7dc.csv). On Ross Ulbricht

Bitcoin owes its popularity to marketplaces that sold illegal things (e.g., Silk Road).

> If you want to know why crypto is so popular today, look at none other than Ross Ulbricht. Yes, Satoshi Nakamoto created the token. What gave it value was Silk Road and Ross Ulbricht.

The marketplace operated successfully for some time before its owner slipped up. Ulbricht made a [post on BitcoinTalk] looking to recruit a person with Bitcoin knowledge into what was obviously Silk Road, and he used his real name in the email address he gave in the post (rossulbricht@gmail.com). This is how law enforcement learned of the identity of the person in charge of Silk Road.

![17432238383412224773276909568443](https://github.com/user-attachments/assets/aef02836-656d-43f1-ab34-31d071c4e782)

> Everybody else trying to break out of prison. You're trying to break into prison.

His arrest involved an undercover DEA agent participating in a scheme where Ulbricht thought he was hiring a hitman. This is similar to the way undercover cops arrest disgruntled husbands willing to hire hitmen to kill their wives.

> Some husband would be reading through Soldier of Fortune magazine. He'd get through the back of the classifieds and he'd see hit man for hire, mercenary for hire, and he'd say, "I hate my wife. I want my wife dead." And he'd contact who he thought was a hit man who was really a law enforcement officer. He'd contact this person, "I want my wife dead." And they'd meet in a park someplace and the cop would be, "You want your wife dead?" "I want my wife dead." "How much money have you got?"

The undercover police officer will demand a small chunk of money for the hit upfront.

> As soon as he pays that,	they arrest the guy, charge him for conspiracy to commit murder, and give the guy a boatload of time, even though his wife was never killed.

Ulbricht was also charged with conspiracy to commit murder, along with drug and money laundering conspiracies.
