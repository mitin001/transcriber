# [28](https://s3.amazonaws.com/writecomments.com/transcripts/933e2045b76bfe5118f3bf2cfcf25c18.csv). Addiction

Brett Johnson confesses that his two addictions in life are toxic relationships and criminal activity. His addiction to crime began at an early age when he had to steal bread from a store so he and his sister wouldn't die of hunger.

> One of the big secrets about crime is, yeah, you may commit crime out of necessity, but it quickly gets outside of necessity.

The shoplifting quickly escalated to include toys and pretty clothes.

> Understand that you are choosing that addiction above everything else, above relationships. You're not loving the other person that's in your life. You're loving that addiction more because you're choosing that every single day, more than you are that person. I've been there. I've done it. It's horrible.

Brett Johnson has been able to recognize this as an addiction, and he has spent many years of his life fighting it. He is now an expert on addiction to crime.

> Every single day you're choosing that addiction more than you are the other people that are important to you because that addiction is more important, which means that you can't love anything except that addiction. That's the love of your life, whatever that is, substance, behavior, whatever that is, that's what you choose. You got to accept that.

Addiction brings immeasurable guilt, but it can be helped with cognitive behavioral therapy.

> If you can accept that, I'll tell you what, you start processing that shit that starts eating away at you.

Acceptance allows one to process their feelings, and only then can one start making the right decisions.

> You start to accept it, then you process it, then you start to act.

Brett Johnson learned this from a mantra of CBT.

> Your thoughts determine your feelings. Your feelings determine your actions.

In terms of action, he suggests finding a person who is a good influence on you and associating with that person.

> You've got to be able to rely on a safety net of individuals that will help look out for you. You've got to surround yourself not with the people that you've been surrounded with but with people who you want to be like. Connect with people who you want to be like, and you'll become like those people. You'll rise to the occasion.

At the same time, disassociating from people bringing you down is equally as important.

> If you associate with shit, you're going to get some of it on you.

With the life of crime behind him, Brett Johnson is now popular in right-wing circles. He has been a guest on both Megyn Kelly and Lex Friedman. He doesn't mind expending some of that political capital to call out right-wingers on their bad behavior. When he saw Jordan Peterson trying to troll plus-sized models on Twitter, he pointed Peterson's motivations for this behavior.

> The reason Peterson says it is because the Twitter algorithms, the YouTube algorithms and all that crap, it games more toward conflict than it does trying to help people and be open and honest.

Right-wingers, learn from this man. You don't have to be horrible to other people, online or in-person. If you're addicted to making money off the enragement algorithm, there is a way out. Accept it, process your feelings about it, and start making better decisions.
