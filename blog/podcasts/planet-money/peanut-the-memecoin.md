# [Peanut the Memecoin](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/1235939360)

There's an app for anything these days. That is, if _anything_ means putting out-of-work celebrities to work. Cameo is a service for monetizing fame. If a celebrity doesn't have any work scheduled for the day, they can work for Cameo that day and still get paid the same.

> Cameo suggested people set their prices by calculating how much they are usually paid per minute based on their salary. Like if you are an NBA star making $25 million a year, you should be making about $200 a minute, which could help you set your rate for a 30-second birthday video.

Some days, Peanut the TikTok squirrel made money this way. However, the viral fame didn't last long. Not because people lost interest but because this beloved squirrel was seized by the New York Department of Environmental Conservation and killed shortly afterward. The Department used rabies testing as an excuse.

> Peanut had bitten someone involved with the investigation. And as part of a protocol to test whether Peanut or Fred had rabies, both of them had been euthanized.

_That's_ when people lost interest. Once the initial shock of losing the beloved animal has worn off, Peanut's custodian Mark made a meme coin commemorating Peanut's death, but no one was interested in buying it.

> Mark was hawking his Justice coin weeks after Peanut's death. The viral curve around the story had already started to flatten.

The attention economy is fast-paced.

> Mark says, if he's learned anything from his journey into the depths of the attention economy, it's how fickle and impermanent the world's attention really is.

The attention economy is also an outgrowth of capitalism, which means its focus is squarely on making a quick buck.

> Mark was speaking largely to people who were just looking to profit.

The capitalist dream of never having to work again is just as strong as it is unattainable. George Carlin would have said you'd have to be asleep to believe it.

> Most of these people are just getting involved in these projects to make money, for the dream of making generational wealth and walking away from your nine-to-five and never looking back.

And yet, desperate people stuck in soul-crushing jobs keep trying to escape their sad realities even as their reported means of such an escape are ridiculous. Meme coins are ridiculous.

> It's the world's biggest Ponzi scheme. Let's not get it twisted. Because whoever buys first makes money off whoever buys next.

The attention economy and meme coins are not for seeking justice.

> Mark seemed to be making a sort of earnest appeal about how switching Peanut memecoins would somehow bring justice for his untimely death.

And yet, Mark has been keeping himself busy if not to get justice for Peanut but at least to get himself through the grieving process.

> I have to focus that anger into motivation and positivity because if I don't, I'm going to just mentally break down myself.
