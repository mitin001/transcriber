# [Trump met crypto](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/1254874796)

The Planet Money team went to a crypto conference. This is a place where [Ross Ulbricht] is celebrated as the messiah of Bitcoin, not condemned as the creator of Silk Road, an online marketplace for drugs, stolen credit cards, and guns.

> No one we talked to objected to this convicted criminal being celebrated.

Bitcoin is the currency criminals use to launder their money. It owes its popularity to Ulbricht's Silk Road. And now that Ulbrichit is a hero and a recipient of a presidential pardon, holding criminal money is all of a sudden considered cool and noble.

> Ulbricht's reversal in fortune tracks with a broader turnaround. Maybe the whole crypto world, its history of fraud and swindles, was being pardoned.

These people are no better than those who celebrate Luigi Mangione gunning down a CEO of United Healthcare because our healthcare system is broken. Picking off random fat cats off the street and shooting them doesn't make healthcare any more affordable. Similarly, giving criminals a free pass because you want people to invest in criminal money so you can cash out on those investments does nothing to reverse the widening income inequality.

> There are a lot of people who rightly feel like work doesn't necessarily translate to wealth these days. And there is a ton of marketing around the cryptocurrency industry that promises people that if they're just clever enough, they can be rich.

Meanwhile, this is an opportunity for our [fraud-in-chief] to do some legal insider trading.

> If you're selling a stock or a bond regulated by the US government, you can't just secretly tip off your friends. That would be insider trading. But meme coins aren't regulated this way, and the Trump administration has said they won't be.
