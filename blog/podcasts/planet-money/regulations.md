# [Regulations](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/1256100344)

No one should have an unfair advantage that lets them make unreasonable demands of their customers. No one should be able to endanger anyone's safety for profit. No one should be able to profit from stolen goods or ideas. Government regulations exist to ensure that these principles are respected and that the system is fair to everyone.

> There are occasional incentives that make it very profitable to do bad things, maybe cheat at the game and steal other people's ideas, or cut some corners on safety. The government, as referee, steps in to make the rules and enforce them. Manage competition in a way that hopefully makes things better for us all.

Regulation is not just there to promote safety and fairness. It must also create an environment that's conducive to innovation. This involves creating proper incentives for the innovators.

> It takes time and money to innovate. So **intellectual property rights** give people and companies some incentive to innovate by providing legal protections against what we may call copycats, just like for land. You may say, well, if someone can take my land tomorrow, why would I plant an expensive crop? People can make the analogous argument for intellectual property rights. If someone can print my book or copy my medicine tomorrow, why should I invest years in developing it?
