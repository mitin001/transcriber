# [The U.S.-China trade war, according to game theory](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/1253382272)

Game theory is a framework that helps us analyze the situation in terms of choices—yours and everyone else's.

> What happens is not only a function of your choices, it's also a function of the choices of others.

Everyone with a stake in a situation makes choices, and your job is to make the correct choice within this web of choices.

> Game theory can show you how strategies bend and shift when I think that you think that I'll do one thing and you think that I think that you'll do this other thing.

Game theory teaches us to identify which game people are playing with you and select the optimal strategy for that game. For example, if you're finding yourself in the prisoner's dilemma, the optimal self-interest strategy is to defect, but if there's trust among the players, the optimal strategy is to cooperate.

> What happens if you think you're playing one game, but, in fact, you're playing a very different game?

Failure to identify the right game significantly reduces your chances of winning.

> If you're playing the optimal strategy to the game that you're not in, the odds that you really have the optimal strategy are very, very low.

And that is, of course, where Trump is with his trade war with China.
