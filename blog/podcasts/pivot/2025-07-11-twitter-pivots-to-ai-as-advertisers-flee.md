# [2025-07-11](https://s3.amazonaws.com/writecomments.com/transcripts/ea128b1045d249f649bbdac03149a02c.csv): Twitter is forced to pivot to AI as advertisers flee the platform

Linda Yaccarino finally left Twitter, after being its CEO-in-name-only for far too long. Elon Musk did not let her do the job of a CEO, and she should've left long ago under citing constructive termination as the reason for quitting.

> If my reporting relationship differs and you put me in the corner and chain me to a desk and tell me I'm now in charge of snacks and not the CEO, that is **constructive termination**.

Under Yaccarino's "leadership", Twitter became an ad-supported platform struggling to get companies to put their ads on the site. This was due in large part to her inability to get content moderation up to snuff, especially as Twitter's new owner Elon Musk got increasingly desperate for the attention of the platforms users that he started encouraging content that was not safe for advertisers to be associated with. With Grok, Twitter became the early adopter of AI, and it might have to become an early adopter of AI-as-a-service, too, if it's still interested in being able pay its bills. Neither Twitter nor AI are safe for advertisers and so both of them have to find other ways to make money. Kara reads excerpts from an essay by [Henry Innis], in which he argues that the unpredictability of AI outputs makes advertisers squirm.

> The cost of guaranteeing brand safety in the age of AI is becoming prohibitively expensive. This is forcing platforms into a choice: either become a fortified, walled garden for advertisers or find a new way to exist entirely.

Scott doesn't see ads in AI's future. Instead, it will be a subscription service. 

> Customized, individualized premium content you pay for.

And you will pay for it because it will make instant decisions for you based on the contents of your mail, calendar, etc. For example, it will book travel and doctor's appointments for you, truly a virtual assistant. This is a bright future, but we must go through growing pains to get there. The person at the helm of innovation in the space is [Elon Musk], a notorious risk-taker.

> Everything he does is minimum viable safety.

[Henry Innis]: https://marketingeconomics.substack.com/p/yaccarino-x-xai-and-why-social-media
