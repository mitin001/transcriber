# [2025-03-04](https://s3.amazonaws.com/writecomments.com/transcripts/aec7b739ce163ec78ed0b34036f1bfd3.csv): loyalty vs. higher wage

Scott Galloway notes that the surest way to sustain a persistent salary growth is to hop jobs every 3-5 years.

> No one's gonna manage your career for you. What you need to do is constantly quit. As a matter of fact, the surveys show the people who make the most money are job switchers, not every year, but every three to five years.

However, there's a way to achieve the same by staying at the same company long-term.

> I have been at NYU for 23 years, but I quit every three to five years. What do I mean by that? I go get an offer from a Columbia or Wharton or somewhere else, and then I walk up to the Dean's office and I say, "This is the offer I have, and I'm totally transparent. I don't wanna leave. I wanna stay, but I need you to match the offer 'cause this is market." And they hum and they haw and they make a bunch of excuses and then they match it.

Interview around, get those jobs, be transparent about it, and turn the offers down once your boss matches them.

> If you don't wanna leave your job, you quit without quitting. You do a market check. You don't be an asshole. You go into your boss and you say, "I got approached. This is what they're offering me. I'd like to stay." Be transparent. The truth has a nice ring to it and get a higher salary.

Do not sacrifice your paycheck for loyalty to your company. Especially when you can get the best of both worlds: be both highly paid and loyal.
