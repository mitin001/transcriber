# [10.03](https://s3.amazonaws.com/writecomments.com/transcripts/017046983dfe2571a38c20d5a0bb28a8.csv). The Other Guy’s Hamburger

9/11 was a catalyst for significant changes in America from TSA to cable news. Security was strengthened while news broadcasts became darker in tone and more urgent in appearance.

> Fox and Roger Ailes did change television on September 11th in a way that has endured. They did it with an innovation so simple that it feels shocking that it wasn't already a TV staple. A block of text scrolling from right to left across the bottom of the screen.

9/11 was when Fox News introduced its famous crawl, which quickly became a mainstay in cable news everywhere.

> The Fox News crawl started two hours after the first plane hit. It kept on going, a never-ending horizontal stream. That ticker would quickly get copied by both CNN and MSNBC.

Alerts started popping up on the screen incessantly as of to promise a steady stream of breaking news that can't be missed, as if the world was forever reeling from a never-ending disaster.

> The crawls used to be used until recently for really important news. For example, tornado warnings.

You would feel that if you changed the channel, you'd miss an important alert. There would be an impending crisis, and you wouldn't know.

> Regardless of what was happening in the world, the Fox News crawl was there to stay. A subtle promise that you'd always know what was happening, so long as you never flipped the channel.

And so the Fox News logo was getting burned into people's screens. The phrase "it could happen to you" was similarly abused to get people to keep watching cable news.
