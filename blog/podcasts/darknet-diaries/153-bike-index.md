# [153](https://darknetdiaries.com/transcript/153/). Bike Index

Bryan from Bike Index tells a story of tracking down a set of stolen bikes. He found that their pictures were uploaded on an online marketplace by some anonymous poster.

> He posted a bike, and it was the usual thirty crop of pictures.

The poster made a stupid mistake. He accidentally uploaded a screenshot of his photo gallery that contained a folder named after his business associate.

> He had accidentally taken a screenshot of the phone, not the picture itself, and it had a name, a very unique name at the top of that phone, that picture, which meant he had a folder that had that very unique name, which meant there’s probably one of his followers that has a name like that. So, we looked at his followers. We found a guy with that name. That guy was located in San Jose.

One of his followers on this online marketplace had the same name and information about his car shop. After examining the photos of the car shop on that page, Bryan could tell that the photos with stolen bikes were taken in that car shop.

> The first thing I see when I open up this car shop’s Facebook page is this San Jose asshole with a bunch of bikes and the same visual settings that we had been seeing.

This lead was a happy accident. There was only a short window of time when the screenshot was left in the listing unnoticed.

> Forty minutes later, he had realized what he had done and he removed that one single photo from his listing. So, it was just this tiny window of time that if I had not eaten my bagel faster that morning and not logged in at that exact point and not — if my day had panned out any differently, I probably would have missed it.

The lesson is, if you see something suspicious, save it for later. You never know if the evidence is still going to be there when you come back.

> That picture was gone, but I had already screenshot it. So, it was this very small, very lucky — complete OSINT win.

Another lesson is to play the long game. Watch your target until they slip up and seize in the opportunity once it presents itself.

> Look at it long enough, somebody’s gonna trip up, and he did.

When confronted, the bike thief went DARVO.

> Deny, accuse, reverse-victim offender.

The thief said that journalists that claim to have exposed him actually made up sensational claims to drive traffic to their website and make money.
