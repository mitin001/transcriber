# [98](https://darknetdiaries.com/transcript/98/). Zero Day Brokers

Cybersecurity reporter for the NY Times, Nicole Perlroth, explains her findings from the Snowden documents. She reports that the NSA knew that the ciphers commonly in use by encryption products could be circumvented, and the agency used it to their advantage whenever they needed access to information that someone wanted to keep secret.

> The NSA could break through this essentially weak random number generator and were pushing the international standard bodies that set encryption standards to use this weak random number generator that the agency could break.

Allegedly, the RSA was effectively bribed by the government into adopting these weak ciphers as their security standards.

> It appeared that the NSA might have actually been paying RSA to bake these weak number generators into some of their security products.

Herein lies a paradox. The US government will always violate the privacy and security of the individual American people in the name of "national security."

> The US government was pushing this vulnerable system into commercial products because it enabled them to conduct espionage.  Once again, it’s just another example of the trade-off that the US was willing to make in the name of national security but would have left Americans more vulnerable.
