# [106](https://darknetdiaries.com/transcript/106/). @Tennessee

> We’re living in a time where our personal information is spilling all over the place, polluting the internet, and it’s going to be incredibly hard to fix when the next generation wakes up and demands this stop.

Open-source intelligence gathering has become child's play. You can often find someone's personal information by searching just their name on the Internet. You will often uncover details they'd rather keep private.

> If you have someone’s full name, you can probably pop it into a people search and get tons of information on it.

Bad actors can exploit this to deliberately put their victims in grave danger. If a black hat wants something from you, even something as trivial as your username on a social networking site, he may extort you with credible threats of dispatching the police on you. He knows where you live, and he can swat you. He will impersonate you to the police, making it sound like you're in the middle of committing monstrous crimes, prompting them to blaze toward your home and seriously consider the possibility of having to shoot you on sight. One such victim of swatting, Mark Herring, died from a heart attack during such a police standoff.

> He died because someone was trying to bully him to give up his Twitter account so they could make a few thousand dollars on it.  The police found no dead woman in the house or pipe bombs, and figured out this was a fake emergency.
