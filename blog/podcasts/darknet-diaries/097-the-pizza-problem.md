# [97](https://darknetdiaries.com/transcript/97/). The Pizza Problem

Miles shares the story of being harassed into giving up his Twitter username. It all started when he got [SIM-swapped].

> He was driving in his car when he first noticed.  The little icon on his phone that shows the signal strength just said No Service.  He couldn’t make a call.  He couldn’t get data over the network.  It was like his phone had been disconnected.

If your phone suddenly stops working, beware that this could be a sign of you getting hacked. Miles was hacked because someone wanted to steal his Twitter account.

> Police didn’t give a fuck because it’s basically only harassment unless they swat you.

Some harassers go all the way and [impersonate] their target to the police, prompting them to come and attempt to arrest the person under what are, unbeknownst to them, false pretenses. Experienced harrassers don't cross this line. They stay in the safe zone of harrassment: illegal but not worth the trouble for the law enforcement to investigate.

> You’re in the magic middle where it’s too weird for cops and too small for FBI.  It’s a sad reality.  It’s fascinating to me; he says it’s a sad reality like he knows that this is a problem.  It’s a loophole.

Miles and his family members had to endure many harassing phone calls. They had to turn away many unpaid pizza deliveries made in their names. When Miles discovered that anti-harrassment laws are de-facto unenforceable, he had given up what was coveted by the harrasser. They eventually even became friends.
